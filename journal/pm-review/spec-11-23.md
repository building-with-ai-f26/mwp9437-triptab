# Build spec: Mike's 11/23 ski trip test

Written 11/24 by Claude as product manager, from Mike's own notes on setting up the real ski trip on triptab.org the evening of 11/23. This file is the checklist a build session works from and checks its work against. It is the durable list: the runbook's Steps row points here, and calls Mike hasn't answered stay in the runbook's Open calls (C58 to C61), not here.

## How a build session uses this file

1. Read this file, then the runbook's Decided calls for C58 to C61. Build in wave order. Finish and verify one item before starting the next.
2. An item marked **Waits on Cnn** is not built until that call is in Decided calls. If Mike's answer differs from the recommendation written here, rewrite the item's Behavior and Checks here first, in the same pull request.
3. An item is done only when every check under it is ticked, each with its evidence: a test name, a click-through screenshot at 1280 and 390, or an eval result file in `journal/evals/`. Then fill its row in the Completion table at the bottom.
4. Nothing here rewrites or deletes production data. Where a fix needs that, it is listed under Needs Mike's OK and waits.
5. Before the last merge, have a fresh agent read only this file and the diff (not the build session's summary) and list any check that isn't met. Fix what it finds.
6. Fixtures, evals and screenshots use made-up trips, names, amounts and booking codes. Never commit the real booking emails, codes, amounts or friends' names.

## Wave 1

### T1. Flights keep the times on the confirmation

**Problem.**
- [Mike] Flight times from the import were wrong; they should come straight from the confirmation.
- [DB] The AI read them right: the overnight flight's inbox row says it leaves 17:35 on day N and lands 07:25 on day N+1. The return flight (11:00 to 14:15, same day) saved fine.
- [Replay] Reviewing the overnight flight at 8:10 PM, Mike tapped "Add Thu, Feb 4 to the trip" (the flight leaves the day before the trip started), picked Night, added it, then edited the flight by hand at 8:12 PM.
- [Mike, screenshot] The saved flight's dialog shows "Lands in the: Night" for a 7:25 AM landing.
- [Code] `app/src/pages/TripPage.tsx:340-346`: the review keeps `start_time` and `end_time` only when `d.day === d.source.day`, and sets the arrival part from the arrival time only for a same-day arrival. So an overnight flight saves with no arrival part, and a flight whose day changed on the review card loses its times.
- [Claude, 0.6] Cause: those two conditions, together with the trip-extend step changing the draft's day. Reproduce with the fixture in check 2 first.

**Behavior.**
- The times on the confirmation are the plan's times, whatever the review card does to the day. Moving the departure day on the review moves the arrival day by the same number of days and keeps both clock times.
- The arrival part comes from the arrival time on the arrival day (`partOfEndTime` in `app/src/lib/model.ts`). The dialog's Lands on and Lands in the show it.
- Both review paths behave the same: upload or paste, and a forwarded email (inbox review).

**Checks.**
- [ ] Unit tests on the draft-to-plan step: (a) 11:00 to 14:15 same day: Morning to Day, times kept. (b) 17:35 day N to 07:25 day N+1: starts Evening on N, ends Morning on N+1, both times kept. (c) As (b) where N is the day before the trip starts and the reviewer taps Add that day to the trip: same result as (b), and the trip now starts on N. (d) As (b) with the departure moved one day later on the review: both times kept, arrival moved one day too.
- [ ] Click-through with a canned import reply (`e2e/import-flow.cjs`) for (b) and (c), at 1280 and 390: saved with no hand edit, the card reads "Departs 5:35 PM, lands Fri 7:25 AM" and covers Thu Evening, Thu Night and Fri Morning (drawn as T5 decides).
- [ ] The saved flight's dialog shows Lands on Fri and Lands in the Morning.
- [ ] The same (b) case through the inbox review path.
- [ ] `npm run eval:import` shows no drop if anything in the import prompt or schema changed (nothing should need to).

The overnight flight already saved on the live trip has no arrival part. Mike can fix it in its dialog once this ships; no migration.

### T2. Joining a flight or other own-pay plan asks for your own price

**Problem.**
- [Mike] A friend is on the same flight as Mike but likely paid a different amount. This is common in group travel.
- [Replay] The friend on a phone tapped Join on both of Mike's flights (11/23, 9:16 PM), then Done, and left 4 seconds later.
- [DB] Neither flight has a price for the friend; the only expense on each is Mike's own. Nothing asked them for one.
- [Code] A way to log your own price exists (`log_own_line`; the vision lets a Viewer "log their own cost where everyone pays their own"), but nothing offers it when you join. [Claude, 0.6] Check too whether the friend's card shows Mike's price as if it were theirs.
- [DB] The overnight flight is Planned now, though Mike confirmed it at 8:10 PM; its last change is the friend's join. [Claude, 0.5] Joining may drop a confirmed own-pay plan back to Planned for everyone. Verify first.

**Behavior.**
- Joining a plan where everyone pays their own adds you with no price. Right after Join, a small prompt asks "What did you pay?" (optional): amount, currency, Paid with points, and Skip.
- Until you enter one, the card says "your price not logged" for you, and your Money counts nothing for it. Nobody's price is ever copied to someone else.
- Booked state is per person: the card shows "2 of 3 booked" rather than the whole plan losing Confirmed when someone joins. If the drop to Planned turns out to be deliberate, say so here and keep the count anyway.
- Drop removes only your own line.

**Checks.**
- [ ] On TripTab dev with two accounts: B joins A's own-pay flight; B's card never shows A's price as B's, and no expense row exists for B until B enters one.
- [ ] Click-through at 1280 and 390: the prompt appears after Join; B enters 612 USD; B's Money shows 612 and A's still shows A's own price. A second run with Skip shows "your price not logged".
- [ ] A confirmed own-pay plan stays Confirmed when B joins, and shows the booked count.
- [ ] B taps Drop: B's line goes, A's stays.

### T6. Repeat a plan on several days as separate plans

Decided by Mike on 11/23 (his note). It replaces the 11/17 view on the Product Vision tab that one plan covering every day "works well".

**Problem.**
- [Mike, screenshot] A week of skiing is one plan, "Day 1 of 7", and only the first card carries the details.
- Mike wants a placeholder ski day on each day now, then to set each day's mountain later.

**Behavior.**
- In Add, More days lets you pick days: a chip per trip day, plus Every day of a stop. Saving makes one plan per day picked, each with all the details (title, where, parts, times, status, cost settings), and each edited or deleted on its own.
- The new plans share a series id, so a later "change all of them" is possible. That isn't built now.
- If a cost is entered, each day gets it (per day) unless the user picks one total.
- An existing multi-day activity or meal gets a Split into separate days button in its dialog. The user runs it; there is no migration.
- Flights, transport and stays still span, because each is one booking.

**Checks.**
- [ ] Unit test: seven days picked gives seven plans with the same fields on seven days.
- [ ] Click-through at 1280 and 390: making a ski day over seven days gives seven full cards. Changing day 3's place leaves the others alone. Deleting day 5 leaves six.
- [ ] Split on an existing seven-day plan gives seven plans; the original goes; who's in and opt-ins carry over.
- [ ] The migration (a nullable series id) is additive, runs on TripTab dev first, and the RLS proof still passes.

## Wave 3

### T7. A payment schedule on a plan (deposits and installments)

**Problem.**
- [Mike] The lodging is paid in two installments and only the first is paid. This is common, and TripTab should handle it.
- [DB] The lodging is one expense marked paid in full by Mike.
- [Mike, screenshot] The booking shows a payment schedule: two rows, each with a date and amount, the first ticked.

**Behavior.**
- A plan's cost can have several payment rows, each with an amount, currency, due date, paid or not, and who pays. The plan's cost is their sum.
- Each row splits the way the plan does.
- The card's money mark is the green "$ ✓" only when every row is paid. Until then it reads "1 of 2 paid · next due Dec 11".
- Each unpaid row with a due date makes a to-do, "Pay <plan>: payment 2", for its payer, due that day. Marking the row paid ticks the to-do, and ticking the to-do marks the row paid.
- In Money, paid rows count as spent and unpaid rows as coming up. A foreign row uses the day's rate on the day it is paid.
- Import: when a confirmation shows a payment schedule, propose the rows. That is a prompt change, so it waits on the eval check below.
- Prefer extending `expenses` (already linked to a plan by `item_id`) with a due date and a paid flag over a new table. The migration is additive.

**Checks.**
- [ ] Unit tests: cents add up exactly across rows and splits; each foreign row is converted on its own date.
- [ ] Click-through at 1280 and 390: a stay of 3,000 EUR as 1,000 paid on 11/18 and 2,000 due 12/11 shows "1 of 2 paid", and a to-do for the payer due 12/11 appears. Marking it paid shows "$ ✓" and ticks the to-do.
- [ ] Money shows 1,000 EUR (converted) as spent and 2,000 EUR as coming up.
- [ ] Plans with one expense look exactly as before.
- [ ] Import eval: a new made-up case with a two-row schedule passes, and the other cases don't drop.
- [ ] The RLS proof passes.

### T10. Ask AI: a chat that offers a few options

**Waits on C59 and C60.** This un-parks the "floating chat" parked until after the course; the recommendation is a panel, not a floating bubble.

**Problem.**
- [Mike] Asking Plan with AI for a fondue dinner gave one option. He wants a few, each with the lowdown, then to choose which, when and what time. If it needs a reservation, that should go on the to-do list.
- [DB] That call returned 1 idea in 6.6 seconds; the five minutes before Add was Mike deciding, not waiting.
- [Code] `supabase/functions/plan-with-ai` fills empty slots with one idea per slot.

**Behavior (recommended).**
- A chat panel. Each ask returns three options, each with: name, why it fits, price level, the best time, whether it needs a reservation, and a source link.
- Pick one: the day and part are filled in and can be changed, and it is added as Suggested. Follow-ups refine the options ("cheaper", "closer to the chalet").
- If an option needs a reservation, offer "Add Book <place> to To do", with an assignee and due date.
- Who sees the chat follows C60.
- The AI rules hold:
  - It runs in an Edge Function, behind the Owner check, on Claude Haiku 5.5 at low effort, with a web search cap.
  - Each turn counts toward the 20 calls a person a day and `AI_DAILY_CAP_USD`.
  - Each turn is logged in `ai_calls` (new feature name `ask`).
  - It never invents a place, never overwrites a plan, and every option has its source.

**Checks.**
- [ ] A 10-case eval (made-up trips), in `app/evals/ask/`, run before Mike sees it. Each reply has three options, each with a source, each in the right city and fitting the ask, with the reservation flag right on the obvious cases, and none duplicating a plan already on the trip. Record the cost per turn in `journal/evals/README.md`.
- [ ] Click-through with a canned reply at 1280 and 390, then one live run.
- [ ] A reservation becomes a to-do with the chosen assignee.
- [ ] The chat's storage follows C60 under RLS: a second account can't read it (add the case to `e2e/rls-proof.cjs`).
